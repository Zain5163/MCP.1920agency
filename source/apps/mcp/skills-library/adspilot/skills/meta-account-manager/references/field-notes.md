# Field notes: what running real accounts has taught

Lessons from accounts AdsPilot has managed, written so any business can use them.
Each note is one line: what was tried, what happened with the numbers, and what to
do differently. Businesses are described by type and market, never by name.

**How to read these.** One account is evidence, not proof. Weigh a note by how much
was spent and for how long, and prefer a note that has been confirmed on a second
account. Where a note and the method in SKILL.md disagree, SKILL.md wins until the
note has been confirmed and SKILL.md updated.

**How to add one.** Under "Field notes" below, newest last:

`- YYYY-MM-DD · <business type, market> · <what was tried> → <result with numbers: spend, results, cost per result, days> · <the lesson, one sentence>`

Only add a note when the numbers support it (roughly 3× the target cost spent, or
3+ days). "Too early to tell" is not a note.

## Field notes

- 2026-10-06 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · Audit of 9 months of data → the same product (Chelsea boots) cost about PKR 600 per purchase in February and about PKR 1,350 in September; the winter ads used one clear flat price ("every pair only Rs. 4,499, no extra charges"), dark product posters, and ran in boot season · Seasonal product plus a single clear price was worth more than any targeting change; lead with the in-season product and a simple price.
- 2026-10-06 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · Audit → one ad carried about 85% of a week's spend at frequency 3.1, and sales fell whenever it tired · An account resting on one ad is fragile; keep 4–6 genuinely different concepts live so delivery has somewhere to go.
- 2026-10-06 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · Audit → 13+ ad sets at about PKR 550/day each never left learning, while the one broad ad set did · Fewer, larger ad sets; test new creative inside the broad ad set, not in new small ones.
- 2026-10-06 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · Audit → cost caps tried at PKR 550–700 per result spent only PKR 2,000–4,000 in total before stalling · A cost cap at or below the target in an account averaging 2× that target starves delivery; fix creative and offer first.
- 2026-10-06 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · Audit → a failed card payment stopped delivery for 16 hours, and someone then paused the test campaigns, cutting spend about 60% · Check payment status first in every daily review; a sudden drop is often billing, not ads.
- 2026-10-06 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · Audit → add-to-cart fell from 5.9% to 3.5% of landing-page visitors over seven months while ad click-through held · When cost per purchase rises with steady click-through, look at the store (offer, trust signals, contradictory delivery terms, stale banners) before the ads.
- 2026-10-06 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · Audit → a live, winning ad was showing a leftover AI drafting note and an outdated price to customers · Read every live ad's full text during review; AI-written variants can carry instructions and stale prices into public copy.
- 2026-10-08 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · A Sales campaign (Purchase event named, no optimisation goal named) was created optimising for link clicks; plan review, approval summary and creation all "passed"; the owner spotted it in Ads Manager before activation · Read every created campaign back and compare the optimisation goal with the objective before asking for activation (now automatic in AdsPilot: verify_campaign, and activate_campaign refuses on failure).
- 2026-10-08 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · Funnel comparison, best month vs latest → CPA 636 → 1,398 while checkout completion improved (47% → 58%); CPM +37%, link CTR 2.34% → 1.76%, add-to-cart per visit 5.9% → 3.5% · Diagnose by funnel step before changing anything: here the fixes were hook/offer and the store, not checkout or targeting.
- 2026-10-08 · PK footwear e-commerce, COD · 90-day ad ranking → the lowest cost per purchase (PKR 607, then 743, 1,024) came from video and multi-image ads, while a new round had been planned as static images only · Rank history before planning creative; carry proven formats forward instead of starting from zero.
