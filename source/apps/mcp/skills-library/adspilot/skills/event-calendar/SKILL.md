# Event calendar: plan around the moments people already buy

Seasons and events move demand on every channel (paid ads on any platform, organic
posts, email, SMS and WhatsApp). The dates are fixed or predictable, so missing one
is a planning failure, not bad luck. The full dated calendar, with sources, is
`references/calendar-2026-2027.md`.

## How to use it

1. **Know where the business sells and what.** Countries and cities, product category,
   and whether it ships (shipping cut-offs move every deadline earlier).
2. **Look 6–8 weeks ahead** in the calendar for those countries and that category.
   Do this in every weekly review, not only when asked. Canada, Australia and New Zealand
   have no dated section yet: use the shared dates (Black Friday, Christmas, Boxing Day,
   Mother's and Father's Day rules in section 2) and confirm local ones (for example
   Australia's end-of-financial-year sales in June) with the owner or an official
   holiday page before planning.
3. **For each relevant event, decide:** run it, adapt it, or skip it. Not every event
   fits every business; a footwear store gains from wedding season and White Friday,
   not from Earth Day.
4. **Work back from the launch-by date**: new ads need several days to leave Meta's
   learning phase, so event creative goes live 1–3 weeks before the peak (the calendar
   gives a launch-by date for each). Prepare creative a week before that.
5. **Get the offer from the business.** Never invent a discount, bundle or free gift.
   Propose options; the owner decides. How a sale may be shown differs by country (in the
   EU a "was" price must be the lowest of the previous 30 days; countdowns must be true
   everywhere): check `get_skill selling-by-country` for the market before building
   banners and landing pages.
6. **Plan the budget curve**: ad prices (CPM) rise around big sale periods (late
   October to mid-December is the costliest stretch); start earlier at a steady budget
   rather than late at a high one, and expect cheaper delivery after the peak.
7. **Check sensitivities before scheduling anything** (below).
8. **After the event**, add what happened to the account's field notes: which angle,
   offer and timing worked, with numbers.

## Channels

| Channel | Use the calendar to |
|---|---|
| Paid ads (Meta, TikTok, Google, Snapchat, Pinterest…) | Launch event creative by the launch-by date, raise budget gradually into the peak, retire it right after |
| Organic social (AdsPilot publishing) | Teasers 1–2 weeks out, greetings on the day, results or thanks after |
| Email / SMS / WhatsApp | Early-access messages to past buyers before the public sale; reminders before shipping cut-offs |

## Sensitivities (check every time)

- **Mourning and solemn days**: in Pakistan and Shia-majority markets, pause sale,
  music-led, celebratory and wedding creative around Muharram and fully on 9–10
  Muharram (Ashura). National days of remembrance and days of national tragedy call for
  respectful messages only.
- **Religious occasions**: Ramadan creative should be respectful (timing around iftar
  and suhoor, no eating or drinking imagery in daytime contexts); Eid greetings are
  welcome, hard-sell on the day of Eid prayers is not.
- **National days** (14 August, 6 September, 23 March, Quaid-e-Azam Day, Iqbal Day):
  patriotic and tribute themes work; not every one is a discount hook (the calendar
  says which).
- **Lunar dates move by moon sighting**: treat them as estimates (the calendar gives the
  range) and confirm a few days before.
- When two events collide (for example a religious day falling on a national holiday),
  prepare a respectful version of the creative and use it.

## Rules for later years

The calendar's section 2 gives the rule for each date (fixed day, "4th Thursday of
November", lunar estimate), so future dates can be computed. Lunar Islamic dates move
about 11 days earlier each Gregorian year.
