# Canada

**Updated 2026-10-08.** Practical guidance, not legal advice: consumer protection is
provincial, so confirm anything binding with a lawyer in the buyer's province.
**V** = read on the official page on 2026-10-08; **R** = reported by a secondary source or
search summary; **Est.** = industry data.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Currency | Canadian dollar. English: $12.99 (often "CA$" or "CAD" when selling across borders); French (Quebec): 12,99 $ |
| Price display | Price **before sales tax**, tax added at checkout; every fixed mandatory fee **in** the price (drip pricing is illegal) (V) |
| Sales tax | GST 5% everywhere; HST 13% (Ontario), 14% (Nova Scotia, since 1 April 2025), 15% (New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, PEI) (V); QST 9.975% in Quebec (R); BC, Manitoba and Saskatchewan add provincial sales tax |
| Returns | No general cooling-off right for online purchases (Ontario: V); provincial disclosure rules for internet contracts |
| Language | English; **French required for Quebec** |
| How people pay | Credit cards, wallets, Interac debit, buy now pay later |

## How people pay, and what to show at checkout

- In 2025 Canadian e-commerce, credit cards were about 46% of value, digital wallets
  (Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal) about 32% and BNPL about 5% (Global Payments Report, R, Est.).
- Show card logos, wallets, and where offered Interac, Shop Pay Installments (Affirm;
  available to Canadian businesses, $35–$30,000, V), Afterpay, Klarna or Affirm (R).
- Shopify Payments and WooPayments are available to Canadian merchants (V).

## Prices, tax and discounts

- Prices are normally shown before GST/HST/PST, with tax added at checkout; Shopify does
  this for Canada by default (V).
- **Drip pricing is a deceptive practice** (Competition Act, since 23 June 2022): a price
  that cannot be paid because fixed obligatory fees are added later is false or
  misleading; only charges imposed by government, such as sales tax, may be added (V).
- **Environmental claims** must be backed by adequate and proper testing (product claims)
  or substantiation (business claims), since 20 June 2024; private parties may take cases
  to the Competition Tribunal since 20 June 2025 (V). The "internationally recognized
  methodology" requirement was removed in March 2026 (V).
- Penalties for corporations reach the greater of $10 million ($15 million for repeat
  cases) or three times the benefit, or 3% of worldwide revenue (V).
- "Was" prices and urgency claims fall under the Act's general misleading-representation
  rules: keep them true.

## Consumer law that changes pages

- **Internet agreements (Ontario Consumer Protection Act, 2002):** disclose the required
  information before the contract, let the buyer accept, decline or correct errors, and
  send a copy within 15 days; if not, the buyer may cancel within 7 days after receiving
  the copy (R). Other provinces have similar rules (R). The Consumer Protection Act, 2023
  (Ontario) has Royal Assent but is **not in force** (R).
- No general online cooling-off right in Ontario; cooling-off applies only to specific
  contract types (V).
- **Subscriptions:** British Columbia's new subscription and auto-renewal rules apply
  mostly from 1 Aug 2026; Quebec's online-subscription rules from 12 Sept 2026 (R). Show
  terms clearly and allow easy online cancellation.
- Give business name, address and contact details, delivery terms and the returns
  policy clearly.

## Privacy, cookies and tracking

- **PIPEDA** (federal) requires meaningful consent for collecting and using personal
  information in commercial activity; Quebec, British Columbia and Alberta have their own
  similar laws (V).
- **Quebec (Law 25):** technologies that identify, locate or profile a person must be
  **off by default**; the person turns them on, after being told how (V). Tracking and
  ad-profiling cookies need a positive action such as "I accept" (R). For Quebec
  visitors, treat it like the EU: hold the Meta pixel and ad cookies until consent.
- **Anti-spam (CASL):** commercial email and texts need consent (express, or implied for
  2 years after a purchase and 6 months after an inquiry), sender identification and an
  unsubscribe processed within 10 business days (V). Use an unticked box on sign-up forms.

## Accessibility

- **Ontario (AODA):** public websites of businesses and non-profits with **50 or more
  employees** must meet **WCAG 2.0 AA** (with two caption and audio-description
  exceptions) (V).
- The Accessible Canada Act covers federally regulated sectors (banks, telecoms,
  transport), not general online shops (R).
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA (web-ui-design).

## Delivery, returns and trust

- Show delivery times and costs per province; remote areas take longer.
- A clear returns policy (window, condition, who pays shipping) is expected, and required
  in Google Merchant Center listings (V, Google).
- Trust: real reviews, Canadian contact details, prices in CAD (not USD) for Canadian
  visitors.

## Language and localisation

- **Quebec (Charter of the French Language, Bill 96):** catalogues and commercial
  publications, including websites and social media, must be available in French for a
  business marketing in Quebec; a bilingual site is fine if the French is at least as
  available and as good (R; OQLF). Since 1 June 2025, generic and descriptive words in
  trademarks on products must also appear in French (V). Whether this reaches sellers
  based outside Quebec is not settled here: ask a Quebec lawyer.
- Shopify Markets: a Canada market with English and French and CAD pricing.
- Phone fields +1; address with province and postal code (A1A 1A1).

## Ad-policy notes

- Meta special ad categories (housing, employment, financial products and services) have
  been mandatory for Canadian audiences since 3 Dec 2020 (V).
- Ads in French for Quebec audiences, landing pages to match.

## Page checklist

- [ ] Prices in CAD, before tax, with every fixed mandatory fee included.
- [ ] French version for Quebec buyers.
- [ ] Contract information and order confirmation (Ontario internet agreement rules).
- [ ] Quebec: tracking off until the visitor turns it on.
- [ ] CASL-compliant sign-up forms and unsubscribe.
- [ ] Environmental claims backed by testing.
- [ ] AODA (WCAG 2.0 AA) if 50+ employees; WCAG 2.2 AA as the target.
