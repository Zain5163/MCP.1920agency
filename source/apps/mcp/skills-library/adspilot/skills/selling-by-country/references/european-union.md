# European Union (with Germany, France, Netherlands, Spain, Italy)

**Updated 2026-10-08.** Practical guidance, not legal advice: EU directives are applied
through each country's own law, so confirm anything binding with a lawyer in the buyer's
country. **V** = read on the official page on 2026-10-08; **R** = reported by a secondary
source or search summary; **Est.** = vendor or industry data.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Currency | Euro in all five countries covered here. Germany, France, Spain, Italy: `12,99 €` or `12,99 EUR` with a decimal comma and a full stop or space for thousands (1.299,00 € / 1 299,00 €); Netherlands: `€ 12,99`. Let the platform format it |
| Price display | **Final price including VAT** and all taxes; unit price where required (V) |
| Returns | **14-day withdrawal right** for online purchases; **withdrawal button** required since 19 June 2026 (V) |
| Discounts | "Was" price = **lowest price of the previous 30 days** (V) |
| Language | The buyer's language for pre-contract information (French in France, Castilian Spanish in Spain, and so on) |
| How people pay | Varies by country: PayPal and invoice in Germany, iDEAL / Wero in the Netherlands, Cartes Bancaires in France, Bizum in Spain, cards and PayPal in Italy |
| Accessibility | European Accessibility Act applies to e-commerce from 28 June 2025 (V) |

## How people pay, and what to show at checkout

| Country | What buyers expect (R, Est. unless stated) |
|---|---|
| Germany | PayPal (about 30% of online sales), invoice "Kauf auf Rechnung" (about 24%, often through Klarna), direct debit or bank transfer (about 21%), credit cards (about 12%) — EHI Online-Payment 2023 (R) |
| Netherlands | iDEAL, about 71% of online payments (Currence 2025, R). iDEAL is moving to **Wero**: co-branded "iDEAL \| Wero" since early 2026, banks connected and payments moving from October 2026, full migration targeted by end of 2027 (V, EPI / ideal.nl). Show the current iDEAL \| Wero logo your payment provider supplies |
| France | Cards, mostly the domestic Cartes Bancaires scheme (about 64% of card payments in H2 2025, R); PayPal |
| Spain | Cards; **Bizum** (30.6 M users and 105.6 M e-commerce payments in 2025, R); PayPal |
| Italy | Cards (about a third), wallets about 35% (PayPal, then PostePay), bank transfer about 13%, cash on delivery about 4%; BNPL Scalapay and Klarna; Satispay (R, Est.) |

Shopify Payments offers iDEAL | Wero, Bancontact, Klarna, Bizum, Satispay and other local
methods in Europe (V); WooPayments is available to merchants in all five countries (V).
Show the badges of the methods really enabled. Buy now pay later (Klarna and others) is
common; cash on delivery is a minor method. A store selling to several EU countries should
offer each country's main local method, not only cards.

## Prices, tax and discounts

- **Final price including VAT** and all other taxes for consumers, plus a **unit price**
  (per kg, litre, m, m², m³) where required (Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC) (V).
  Germany: Grundpreis rules in § 4 PAngV, with small exemptions (V).
- **Delivery costs** shown before the order; in Spain the site must say whether taxes are
  included and give shipping costs (LSSI art. 10) (V).
- **Discounts (Omnibus Directive 2019/2161, art. 6a of 98/6/EC, from 28 May 2022):** when
  announcing a price reduction, the "prior price" shown is the **lowest price applied in
  at least the 30 days before** the reduction (V). A percentage discount must be
  calculated from that price (CJEU C-330/23 Aldi Süd, 26 Sept 2024) (V). Germany: § 11
  PAngV (V). Some countries exempt perishable goods or new products.
- **Reviews:** say whether and how reviews are checked to come from real buyers; claiming
  "verified reviews" without reasonable checks is unfair (UCPD art. 7(6)) (V).
- **Personalised prices** set by automated decision-making must be disclosed (V).
- **Fake urgency** is an unfair practice. Italy's competition authority fined a retailer
  €2 M in June 2026 for discount countdown timers that simply restarted (V); the Dutch
  ACM's online-consumer guidelines name fake countdowns and scarcity (V).
- **France "soldes":** the word "soldes" may only be used in the two official 4-week sale
  periods a year, online too (V). Use "promotion" outside them.
- **Penalties** for widespread infringements reach at least 4% of annual turnover in the
  countries concerned (V).
- **VAT across borders:** above €10,000 a year of cross-border B2C sales inside the EU, the
  buyer's country's VAT applies, declared through the One-Stop Shop (OSS); imports of
  €150 or less through IOSS (V). From **1 July 2026**, a flat **€3 customs duty per item**
  applies to low-value parcels until mid-2028 (V; sources differ on whether it applies
  to all or only IOSS consignments). Sellers outside the EU: price this into the
  delivered price.

## Consumer law that changes pages

- **14-day withdrawal** (Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU) (V):
  - for goods, from the day the buyer receives them;
  - extended by up to 12 months if the buyer is not told about the right;
  - refund within 14 days using the original payment method (the trader may wait until
    the goods are back unless it collects them);
  - provide the model withdrawal form (Annex I(B));
  - delivery within 30 days unless agreed otherwise.
- **Withdrawal button, since 19 June 2026** (art. 11a CRD, added by Directive 2023/2673)
  (V): a function labelled "withdraw from contract here" or equivalent, prominent and
  available for the whole withdrawal period; the buyer enters name, order details and
  email; the trader confirms receipt on a durable medium (email) without delay. Germany:
  § 356a BGB, buttons "Vertrag widerrufen" then "Widerruf bestätigen" (V). Shopify and
  WooCommerce stores need an app, a page form or a theme change for this; ask the owner
  what their platform provides, and never claim it exists unless it does.
- **Order button** (art. 8(2) CRD): labelled "order with obligation to pay" or equally
  unambiguous (V). Germany: "zahlungspflichtig bestellen" or an unambiguous equivalent,
  otherwise **no contract comes into existence** (§ 312j(3)–(4) BGB) (V).
- **Legal notice (who you are):**
  - Germany, Impressum (§ 5 DDG, replaced § 5 TMG in May 2024): name, address, legal form,
    representatives, fast electronic contact and email, register entry, VAT ID,
    supervisory authority where relevant (V).
  - France, mentions légales: identity, RCS number, email and phone, VAT number, and the
    host's name, address and phone (V); also name the consumer mediator ("médiateur de
    la consommation") with contact details on the site and in the terms (R).
  - Spain, aviso legal (LSSI art. 10): name, address, email, commercial register, NIF tax
    number (V).
  - Italy: the VAT number (Partita IVA) on the home page (art. 35 DPR 633/1972) (R).
  - Netherlands and others: name, address, contact and registration details as part of
    the CRD pre-contract information.
- **Subscriptions in Germany:** a "Verträge hier kündigen" button, then "jetzt
  kündigen"; otherwise the buyer may cancel at any time (§ 312k BGB) (V).
- **Legal guarantee:** at least 2 years for goods (France: art. L217-3 Code de la
  consommation, with information required in the terms) (R). Returns and warranty pages
  must not suggest less.
- **Product safety on listings** (GPSR 2023/988, from 13 Dec 2024, art. 19) (V): each
  online offer shows the manufacturer's name and postal and electronic address; the EU
  responsible person if the manufacturer is outside the EU; product identification with
  a picture; warnings and safety information in the buyer's language.
- **Green claims, from 27 Sept 2026** (Directive 2024/825) (V): generic environmental
  claims ("eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable") without recognised proof, home-made
  sustainability labels, whole-product claims that cover a part, and "climate neutral"
  claims based on offsetting are banned. Italy fined a fashion retailer €1 M for vague
  green claims in 2025 (V).
- **Remove the old EU ODR link:** the Online Dispute Resolution platform closed on
  20 July 2025 and the duty to link to it ended (Regulation 2024/3228) (V).
- **Germany, packaging:** anyone shipping packaged goods to German consumers, including
  foreign sellers, registers in the LUCID packaging register before the first sale and
  joins a packaging scheme (VerpackG) (V).
- **Language:** France requires French for the offer, instructions, guarantee terms and
  invoices (Loi Toubon) (R); Spain requires at least Castilian for pre-contract
  information (R).
- **Coming:** a Digital Fairness Act (dark patterns, subscriptions, influencer marketing)
  is announced for late 2026; no proposal yet (V).

## Privacy, cookies and tracking

- **Prior consent** for any non-essential cookie, pixel or similar access to the device
  (ePrivacy art. 5(3) and GDPR); tracking pixels are covered (EDPB Guidelines 2/2023) (R).
- **Reject as easy as accept:** if "Accept all" is on the first layer, "Reject all" should
  be there too (EDPB cookie banner taskforce, 2023, R); France's CNIL requires refusing to
  be as easy as accepting (V) and fined SHEIN €150 M in Sept 2025 for cookies set before
  consent (V); Italy: closing the banner with "X" means no tracking, scrolling is not
  consent, and do not ask again for 6 months (Garante, 2021, R); Spain's AEPD guide
  (2023) puts accept and reject at the same level (R).
- **Meta pixel:** `fbq('consent', 'revoke')` before the pixel starts on every page, then
  `fbq('consent', 'grant')` after the visitor agrees; Meta states each business is
  responsible for its own GDPR compliance, and its Business Tools Terms (which cover the
  Conversions API) require the consents the law needs (V). Do not send server events
  for visitors who refused (our reading of those terms; Meta has no page saying it in
  those words).
- **Google tags:** Consent Mode v2 with four signals, `ad_storage`, `analytics_storage`,
  `ad_user_data` and `ad_personalization`. Google requires consent signals for users in
  the **EEA, the UK and Switzerland** to keep measurement, remarketing and personalised
  ads (EU user consent policy) (V). Basic mode loads no Google tag until the visitor
  chooses; advanced mode sends cookieless pings when consent is refused (V).
- **On Shopify:** Settings > Customer privacy > Cookie banner. "Use automated settings"
  shows the banner in the UK and EEA when the store has markets there (V). The banner
  governs Shopify's own tools and Shopify pixels; third-party scripts are covered only
  when they use the Customer Privacy API (V). The Google & YouTube app turns Consent Mode
  on automatically once the banner is enabled (V). App pixels such as Meta's run in a
  sandbox and may be restricted without consent (V). Shopify's legal-policy templates are
  English-only and not country-aware: write EU texts with the owner and a local adviser (V).
- **On WordPress:** a consent plugin that blocks tags until consent, plus Consent Mode.
- **Pending:** the Commission's "Digital Omnibus" would move cookie rules into the GDPR;
  still in negotiation, not law (R).

## Accessibility

- **European Accessibility Act** (Directive 2019/882) applies to e-commerce services from
  **28 June 2025** (V). Micro-enterprises providing services (fewer than 10 people and
  turnover or balance sheet of €2 M or less) are exempt (V). National laws set the
  details and penalties.
- The practical standard is EN 301 549, which follows WCAG 2.1 AA; a new version written
  for the EAA was reported adopted in Aug 2026 but not yet cited in the Official Journal
  (R). Target WCAG 2.2 AA (web-ui-design), which covers 2.1 AA.

## Delivery, returns and trust

- Delivery within 30 days unless agreed (V); state real delivery times per country.
- Returns: the 14-day withdrawal right is the legal floor; many stores offer longer
  (30 days) as a selling point (practice, not law).
- Trust marks: Trusted Shops (Germany), Thuiswinkel Waarborg (Netherlands, a yearly
  certification with 2,000+ members, R), Trustpilot widely seen; all reviews must follow
  the review rules above.

## Language and localisation

- One language version per market, translated by a fluent speaker; legal and checkout
  text never left to raw machine translation.
- `hreflang` links between versions (Shopify Markets adds them; on WordPress the
  translation plugin should).
- German and French text runs longer than English: buttons and menus must allow it.

## Ad-policy notes

- **Political, electoral and social-issue ads:** Meta stopped them in the EU from
  6 October 2025, and Google stopped political ads in the EU before the TTPA applied on
  10 October 2025 (V). Ads touching environment, health or other social issues may be
  caught; read the platform playbook.
- Google tags need Consent Mode for EEA traffic (above).
- Landing page language matches the ad's.

## Page checklist

- [ ] Prices include VAT; unit prices where required; delivery costs before the order.
- [ ] "Was" price is the lowest of the previous 30 days; percentage calculated from it.
- [ ] No fake countdowns, scarcity or "people viewing"; no "soldes" outside the periods in France.
- [ ] Withdrawal information, model form, and the **withdrawal button** (since 19 June 2026).
- [ ] Final button "order with obligation to pay" / "zahlungspflichtig bestellen" or equal.
- [ ] Legal notice for the country (Impressum, mentions légales, aviso legal, Partita IVA).
- [ ] Product-safety details on every listing (manufacturer, EU responsible person, warnings).
- [ ] No generic green claims (from 27 Sept 2026); old ODR link removed.
- [ ] Cookie banner blocks pixels until consent; reject as easy as accept; Google Consent Mode.
- [ ] Local payment methods for each country sold to.
- [ ] Germany: LUCID packaging registration done (ask the owner).
