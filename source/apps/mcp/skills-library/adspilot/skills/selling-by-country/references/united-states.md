# United States

**Updated 2026-10-08.** Practical guidance, not legal advice: federal rules plus 50 states'
laws apply, so confirm anything binding (subscriptions, privacy notices, sales tax) with a
US lawyer or tax adviser. **V** = read on the official page on 2026-10-08; **R** = reported
by a secondary source or search summary; **Est.** = industry data.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Currency | US dollar, $12.99; comma thousands ($1,299.00) |
| Price display | Price **before sales tax**; tax added at checkout. California: all mandatory fees in the displayed price (R) |
| Returns | No general federal right to return a purchase; the store's returns policy is a selling point and must be stated truthfully |
| Language | US English (color, center); Spanish versions common for some audiences |
| How people pay | Cards (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover), wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal, Shop Pay), buy now pay later (Affirm, Klarna, Afterpay) |
| Regulators | FTC (federal), state attorneys general, California Privacy Protection Agency |

## How people pay, and what to show at checkout

- In 2025 US e-commerce, digital wallets were about 40% and direct card use about 49%;
  credit outspends debit about 2 to 1 online (Global Payments Report, March 2026) (R, Est.).
  BNPL about 6% (2024, R, Est.).
- Show card logos, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and Shop Pay where enabled. Shop Pay
  Installments (powered by Affirm) is available to US businesses on orders of $35–$30,000
  (V); Affirm, Klarna and Afterpay ("Cash App Afterpay") are the other common BNPL options (R).
- Cash on delivery is not expected; do not add it unless asked.

## Prices, tax and discounts

- **Sales tax** is shown separately and added at checkout. Since *South Dakota v. Wayfair*
  (2018) a state can require remote sellers to collect its sales tax once they pass an
  "economic nexus" threshold; South Dakota's is $100,000 of sales (it dropped its
  200-transaction test in 2023) (V). Thresholds differ by state: the owner checks with
  a tax adviser or a sales-tax service. Shopify shows US prices without tax by default (V).
- **Mandatory fees:** the FTC's fees rule (from 12 May 2025) covers only live-event tickets
  and short-term lodging (V). But **California** (SB 478, from 1 July 2024) requires the
  displayed price to include all mandatory fees except government taxes and reasonable
  shipping (R). Safest everywhere: no surprise fees at checkout.
- **Discounts and urgency:** the FTC's dark-patterns report names countdown timers that
  pretend an offer is time-limited and other false urgency as deceptive; cases are
  brought under section 5 of the FTC Act (V). "Was" prices must be prices genuinely
  charged.

## Consumer law that changes pages

- **Shipping promises** (FTC Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule) (V):
  ship within the time you state, or within **30 days** if you state none; if you cannot,
  tell the buyer promptly, give a new date, and offer to cancel; refunds within 7 working
  days. So only promise delivery times the owner can keep.
- **Reviews** (FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, from 21 Oct 2024) (V): no fake
  or AI-generated reviews or testimonials; no paying for reviews that must be positive (or
  negative); employee and insider reviews only with clear disclosure; no suppressing
  reviews by threats or false accusations; no buying fake followers or views. Civil
  penalties up to $53,088 per violation (V, FTC Dec 2025).
- **Subscriptions and auto-renewal:**
  - The FTC's "click-to-cancel" rule (amended Negative Option Rule, Oct 2024) was
    **vacated by a federal appeals court (8th Circuit) on 8 July 2025** before it took
    effect (R; the date appears in the FTC's own later notice). The FTC published an
    advance notice of new rulemaking in March 2026; no new rule had been proposed by
    October 2026 (V).
  - Still in force: **ROSCA** — before taking billing details online, clearly disclose all
    material terms and get the buyer's express informed consent, and provide a simple way
    to stop recurring charges (V for disclosure and consent; the cancellation mechanism is
    in the statute) — and the original 1973 negative-option rule (V). The FTC keeps
    bringing subscription cases under ROSCA.
  - **California's Automatic Renewal Law** (amended by AB 2863, from 1 July 2025): express
    affirmative consent to renewal terms kept on record, cancellation online the same way
    the buyer signed up, an annual reminder, and limits on "save" offers (R).
    New York, Colorado, Minnesota, Virginia, Massachusetts and others have their own
    auto-renewal laws; New York City (1 Oct 2026) and Louisiana (1 Jan 2027) add
    click-to-cancel rules (R).
  - In practice: show the price, renewal period and how to cancel next to the sign-up
    button; an unticked consent box; cancellation online in as few steps as sign-up.

## Privacy, cookies and tracking

- No federal cookie-consent law. Instead, about **20 states** have comprehensive privacy
  laws in force by 2026 (IAPP tracker, R).
- **California (CCPA/CPRA)** applies to for-profit businesses with gross revenue over
  **$26.625 million** (from 1 Jan 2025), or that buy, sell or share personal information
  of 100,000+ California consumers or households, or earn half their revenue from selling
  or sharing it (V). Such businesses need a "**Do Not Sell or Share My Personal
  Information**" (or "Your Privacy Choices") link, and must treat the browser's **Global
  Privacy Control** signal as an opt-out (V). Ad pixels used for cross-context behavioural
  advertising (for example Meta retargeting) count as "sharing" (V). New CPPA regulations
  apply from 1 Jan 2026 (risk assessments) and 1 Jan 2027 (automated decision-making) (V).
- **On Shopify:** Settings > Customer privacy > Data sharing opt-out page lets visitors
  opt out of sale, sharing and targeted advertising and respects Global Privacy Control;
  it needs a published privacy policy (V).
- Even a small store below the thresholds: publish a privacy policy that names the ad
  platforms and analytics used, and ask the owner before adding tracking.
- **Marketing email** (CAN-SPAM): truthful header and subject, identify the message as an
  ad, a valid postal address, a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days (V).
- **Marketing texts** (TCPA): prior express written consent for automated marketing texts,
  and honour "STOP" (R). Keep SMS consent as a separate, unticked box.

## Accessibility

- The ADA covers businesses open to the public, and the Department of Justice says this
  includes their websites, but there is **no federal web regulation for private
  businesses**; DOJ points to WCAG as helpful guidance (V, 2022 guidance). The 2024 WCAG
  2.1 AA rule applies to state and local governments only, with deadlines moved to
  April 2027 and April 2028 by a 2026 interim rule (R).
- Lawsuits are frequent: 3,117 federal website-accessibility suits in 2025 (Seyfarth) and
  over 5,000 digital accessibility suits counting key state courts (UsableNet) (R, Est.).
  Online stores are common targets. Meeting WCAG 2.2 AA (web-ui-design) is the practical
  defence; overlay widgets are not a substitute.

## Delivery, returns and trust

- State delivery times you can keep (the FTC rule above); show shipping cost before checkout.
- A clear, easy returns policy (window, who pays return shipping, refund timing) is
  expected by shoppers and required in Google Merchant Center listings (V, Google).
- Trust: real reviews (under the FTC rule), recognisable payment badges, a US contact
  route, secure checkout.

## Language and localisation

- US spelling and units (inches, pounds, US shoe and clothing sizes alongside EU/UK where
  relevant). Dates as MM/DD/YYYY; write the month in words to avoid confusion abroad.
- Phone fields accept (555) 123-4567 and +1 formats; address with state and ZIP code.

## Ad-policy notes

- **Special ad categories on Meta** (housing, employment, financial products and services,
  and social issues, elections or politics) are mandatory for US audiences (financial
  products and services since 21 Jan 2025) and limit targeting (V). Read the playbook.
- Health and wellness advertisers in the US face limits on lower-funnel optimisation and
  on custom conversions that suggest health conditions (R); check `get_playbook meta-ads`
  before relying on purchase optimisation.
- Google: destination pages must work, be crawlable and match the ad; the offer in the ad
  must be on the page with the full cost disclosed (Misrepresentation policy) (V).

## Page checklist

- [ ] Price before tax with "tax calculated at checkout"; no surprise mandatory fees (California).
- [ ] Delivery time you can keep; delay process ready (FTC mail order rule).
- [ ] Returns policy stated plainly; Google Merchant Center return policy matches.
- [ ] Only real reviews; insider reviews disclosed; nothing suppressed.
- [ ] No fake countdowns, scarcity or "people viewing".
- [ ] Subscriptions: terms next to the button, express consent, online cancellation, reminders.
- [ ] Privacy policy; "Do Not Sell or Share" link and GPC where state laws apply.
- [ ] Email and SMS consent and unsubscribe.
- [ ] WCAG 2.2 AA.
