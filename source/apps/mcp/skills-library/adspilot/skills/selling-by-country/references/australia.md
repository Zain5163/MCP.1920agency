# Australia

**Updated 2026-10-08.** Practical guidance, not legal advice: confirm anything binding
with an Australian lawyer. **V** = read on the official page on 2026-10-08; **R** =
reported (several Australian regulator sites blocked automated reading, so many ACCC and
ATO points come from search results on the official domain); **Est.** = vendor data.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Currency | Australian dollar, $12.99 (A$ or AUD when selling across borders) |
| Price display | **Single total price** including GST (10%) and every unavoidable fee (R) |
| Returns | Consumer guarantees by law; "no refunds" signs are unlawful; change-of-mind returns are store policy only |
| Card surcharges | **Banned** on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa cards from **1 Oct 2026** (V) |
| Language | Australian English (colour, centre) |
| How people pay | Cards (debit dominant), Apple Pay and Google Pay, PayPal, buy now pay later (Afterpay, Zip, Klarna) |

## How people pay, and what to show at checkout

- Cards were about 73% of all consumer payments in 2025 (debit 49%, credit 23%); PayPal,
  BPAY and BNPL about 2% or less each (RBA Consumer Payments Survey, R; all payments,
  not only online).
- Show card logos, wallets, PayPal, and Afterpay, Zip or Klarna where offered. Shopify
  Payments and WooPayments are available to Australian merchants (V).
- **No surcharges** on eftpos, Mastercard or Visa debit, prepaid and credit cards from
  1 Oct 2026 (Reserve Bank decision, March 2026) (V). Remove any "card fee" line for
  those cards; build costs into prices instead. Other methods (for example Amex, BNPL,
  PayPal) are outside the RBA decision; check before charging anything extra.
- BNPL is regulated credit since 10 June 2025; the licence sits with the provider, not
  the merchant (V, ASIC).

## Prices, tax and discounts

- **Single price rule** (Australian Consumer Law s 48): show the total minimum price as one
  figure, including GST and any unavoidable fees; a part price may appear only if the
  total is at least as prominent. Delivery fees need not be in the price but must be
  disclosed (R, ACCC). Prices shown only to businesses may exclude GST.
- **Was/now and urgency:** the ACCC's sweeps have flagged misleading countdown timers,
  false "limited time" claims, "up to X% off" when few items are, and misleading was/now
  and strikethrough prices; retailers have paid penalties or refunds (R). Keep every
  "was" price and deadline real.
- **Coming 1 July 2027:** the Unfair Trading Practices Act 2026 bans manipulative design
  (dark patterns), requires transaction-based fees to be shown clearly near the base
  price, and adds subscription rules including an easy way to cancel (passed July 2026,
  R). Build to it now.

## Consumer law that changes pages

- **Consumer guarantees** (ACL): goods must be of acceptable quality, fit for any purpose
  the buyer told you about, and match their description; services with due care and
  skill (V). For a major failure the buyer chooses a refund or replacement; for a minor
  one the business may repair (R).
- **Never** write "No refunds" or "No refunds on sale items": such signs are misleading
  and unlawful. "No refunds for change of mind" is acceptable (R, ACCC). A store policy
  can never take away the guarantees (V).
- **Reviews:** no fake reviews, undisclosed incentivised reviews, or suppressing or editing
  negative reviews (ACCC online reviews guidance, R).
- **Unfair contract terms** in standard consumer contracts (cancellation fees,
  auto-renewals) are an ACCC priority for 2026–27 (R).

## Privacy, cookies and tracking

- **Privacy Act 1988:** most businesses with annual turnover of **AUD 3 million or less
  are exempt**, unless they trade in personal information, provide health services, or
  fall in other listed groups (V). Ask the owner; many small stores are exempt, but
  following the rules is still good practice and a pending reform may change this.
- **Tracking pixels** (OAIC guidance, Nov 2024): not banned, but businesses covered by the
  Act must collect only what they need, describe pixels in their privacy policy and
  collection notices, never send sensitive (for example health) information without
  consent, and give a simple opt-out from targeted marketing (V). In June 2026 the
  Commissioner found two health businesses breached the Act through pixels (V).
- No general cookie-banner law, but say what is tracked and why.
- A statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy started 10 June 2025 (V); privacy
  policies must describe significant automated decisions from 10 Dec 2026 (R). A further
  reform bill (exposure draft, Aug 2026) is not law (V).
- **Spam Act 2003:** commercial email and SMS need consent, sender identification and a
  free unsubscribe honoured within 5 working days (R, ACMA).

## Accessibility

- The Disability Discrimination Act 1992 applies to websites; the Human Rights
  Commission's guidance points to WCAG (R; newer 2025 guidelines reportedly recommend
  WCAG 2.2 AA). Target WCAG 2.2 AA (web-ui-design).

## Delivery, returns and trust

- Australia Post: Parcel Post generally 2–6 business days, Express Post 1–3 (R).
- Shoppers: 73% say good delivery makes them more likely to buy online, 69% want more
  delivery options, and the average free-shipping threshold is about A$135 (Australia
  Post eCommerce Report 2026, Est.).
- Trust: real reviews, Afterpay or other BNPL badges if offered, an Australian contact
  route, and a returns page that states the guarantees correctly.

## Selling into Australia from abroad

- Overseas sellers with Australian sales of A$75,000 or more in 12 months collect 10% GST
  on goods worth A$1,000 or less at the point of sale (R, ATO). Prices shown to
  Australian consumers must include it.

## Ad-policy notes

- Read the platform playbook; special ad categories on Meta are optional for audiences
  outside the US, Canada and Europe (V) but restricted goods (health, alcohol, gambling)
  still apply.
- Ad prices follow the single price rule.

## Page checklist

- [ ] One total price including GST and unavoidable fees; delivery cost disclosed.
- [ ] No card surcharge on eftpos, Mastercard or Visa (from 1 Oct 2026).
- [ ] Returns page: guarantees stated; no "no refunds"; change-of-mind policy as an extra.
- [ ] Real was/now prices and deadlines; ready for the 2027 unfair-practices rules.
- [ ] Privacy policy describing pixels (if covered by the Act; good practice anyway).
- [ ] Spam Act consent and unsubscribe.
- [ ] WCAG 2.2 AA.
