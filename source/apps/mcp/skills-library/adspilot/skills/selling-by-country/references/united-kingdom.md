# United Kingdom

**Updated 2026-10-08.** Practical guidance, not legal advice: confirm anything binding with
a UK solicitor or Trading Standards. **V** = read on the official page on 2026-10-08;
**R** = reported by a secondary source; **Est.** = vendor data or estimate.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Currency | Pound sterling, £12.99; comma thousands (£1,299.00) |
| Price display | **VAT and every compulsory charge included** in the price shown to consumers (V) |
| Returns | 14-day cancellation for online purchases, plus Consumer Rights Act remedies for faulty goods (V) |
| Language | English (UK spelling: colour, centre, catalogue); Welsh is optional |
| How people pay | Debit cards dominate; wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal); buy now pay later (Klarna, Clearpay, PayPal Pay in 3) |

## How people pay, and what to show at checkout

- Cards are the base: debit cards were 54% of all UK payments in 2025 and all cards 64%;
  65% of adults are registered for a mobile wallet (UK Finance, Aug 2026; all payments,
  not only online) (R).
- Online, wallets were about 40% of e-commerce value, cards about 46% and BNPL about 8%
  (Worldpay Global Payments Report, from a search summary) (R, Est.).
- Show: card logos, Apple Pay / Google Pay, PayPal, and BNPL if offered. Cash on delivery
  is not expected; do not add it unless asked.
- **BNPL is regulated since 15 July 2026** ("Deferred Payment Credit", FCA) (V). Promoting
  BNPL on the site or in ads is a financial promotion: use the BNPL provider's approved
  wording and widgets, and check its merchant terms. Whether a merchant needs approval or
  an exemption depends on the case (R) — ask the provider, and a lawyer if unsure.

## Prices, tax and discounts

- **Price Marking Order 2004:** the selling price is the final price including VAT and
  other taxes, unambiguous and easy to read; it applies online. Amendments in force from
  6 April 2026 standardise unit prices (per kg, litre, item, m…) and require a unit price
  for promotional and loyalty prices too (unit pricing mainly affects grocery-type goods;
  small shops under 280 m² have an exemption) (V).
- **Drip pricing is banned** (DMCC Act 2024): the headline price must include all
  mandatory fees and charges. Optional delivery charges must be stated, or "may be
  payable" if they cannot be worked out in advance. CMA guidance CMA209 *Price
  transparency* (Nov 2025) has a "3-step pricing check" (V).
- **Ads (CAP Code s.3):** prices quoted include non-optional taxes and charges (3.18);
  delivery charges stated (3.20); "free" only when nothing beyond unavoidable delivery is
  paid (3.23–3.25); the basis of a comparison clear (3.38); an RRP comparison misleads if
  the product is not generally sold at that RRP (3.39) (V).
- **"Was" prices:** the usual reference is the CTSI *Guidance for Traders on Pricing
  Practices* (2016): a "was" price is likely fair only if the product sold at it for at
  least as long as the sale price runs (R). The government announced (9 Aug 2026) a
  consultation on banning fake "was" prices and invented discounts outright (pending, V).
- **False urgency is banned outright:** falsely saying a deal is available only for a
  limited time (DMCC Sch. 20 para 7). A countdown that restarts, or the same deal coming
  back after the "deadline", is the CMA's example (V). In May 2026 a court endorsed the
  CMA's case against Emma Sleep over countdown timers, "high demand" messages and
  discount claims (V).

## Consumer law that changes pages

- **14-day cancellation** (Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013) (V):
  - for goods, it runs from the day the buyer receives them;
  - if the buyer is not told about the right, it extends by up to 12 months;
  - refund within 14 days of getting the goods back (or proof of sending), including the
    outbound delivery up to the cheapest standard option;
  - the buyer pays return postage only if told so beforehand;
  - exceptions include personalised or made-to-order goods, perishables, sealed hygiene
    goods once unsealed, and sealed audio, video or software once unsealed.
- **Information before the order** (Schedule 2): main features, the trader's identity,
  address and contact details, total price with taxes and delivery, payment and delivery
  arrangements, cancellation rights and the model form, who pays returns, the legal
  guarantee of conformity, and complaint handling (V). Put these on product, delivery,
  returns and contact pages and in the order confirmation.
- **Order button:** the buyer must explicitly acknowledge that the order means paying; the
  button is labelled only "order with obligation to pay" or an equally unambiguous
  wording (Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, reg 14(3)–(4)) (V). Wording such as
  "Pay now" is commonly used as equivalent; "Continue" or "Submit" is not.
- **Faulty goods** (Consumer Rights Act 2015): goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit
  for purpose and as described; 30-day right to reject; then repair or replacement, then
  a price reduction or final rejection; faults within 6 months are presumed present at
  delivery (V). A returns policy must not suggest less.
- **Fake reviews are banned** (DMCC Sch. 20 para 13; CMA guidance CMA208): no fake or
  hidden-incentive reviews, no hiding negative ones, and reasonable steps to prevent fakes.
  This covers embedded Trustpilot, Feefo or Google widgets. The CMA opened five review
  cases in March 2026 (including suppressing 1-star reviews and discounts for 5-star
  reviews) (V).
- **Penalties:** the CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover since 6 April 2025 (V).
- **Subscriptions:** the new subscription contracts regime (clear up-front information,
  reminders, easy exit, a cooling-off period after trials and renewals) is announced to
  start in **January 2027** (government, 9 Aug 2026) (V). Build subscription sign-ups and
  cancellation to that standard now, and re-check the final rules before launch.

## Privacy, cookies and tracking

- **Consent before** any non-exempt cookie or tracker; nothing pre-ticked; rejecting as
  easy as accepting; withdrawing as easy as giving (ICO, PECR guidance) (V).
- **Exceptions since 5 Feb 2026** (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025): strictly necessary,
  communication, statistical (analytics used only to improve the service, with clear
  information and a simple free way to object), appearance and functionality, emergency
  help (ICO guidance) (V). **Advertising pixels (Meta, Google Ads, TikTok) always need
  consent** (V).
- The ICO checked the top 1,000 UK sites in 2025 (ad cookies before consent, reject as
  easy as accept) and continues testing in 2026 (V).
- Google tags: use Consent Mode (see `european-union.md`, section on Google, which also
  covers the UK). Meta pixel: load only after consent.
- Marketing email and SMS: consent, or the "soft opt-in" for existing customers, with an
  unsubscribe in every message (PECR; ICO) — use an unticked box for new sign-ups.

## Accessibility

- All service providers must make reasonable adjustments for disabled people under the
  Equality Act 2010, including on websites; there is no statutory WCAG standard for
  private businesses (the public sector must meet WCAG 2.2 AA) (V). A new EHRC Code of
  Practice (2026) says the duty applies to services on websites and is anticipatory (R).
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA (web-ui-design); it is the standard a court or regulator will look to.

## Delivery, returns and trust

- Royal Mail First Class target: 90% next working day; Second Class within 3 working days,
  delivered on alternate weekdays since July 2025 (Ofcom, July 2025) (R).
- Shoppers care about delivery cost: in a 2023 vendor survey 69% chose free delivery over
  speed and nearly three-quarters avoid retailers that charge for returns (nShift) (Est.).
  Show the free-delivery threshold and the returns cost plainly.
- Trust: real reviews (Trustpilot, Feefo, Google are common, R), a UK contact address and
  phone, clear returns. All reviews under the fake-review rules above.

## Selling into the UK from abroad

- Consignments of **£135 or less**: the seller charges UK VAT at the point of sale (the
  marketplace does, when selling through one); above £135, import VAT and duty at the
  border (HMRC) (V). Prices shown to UK buyers must include that VAT.
- The £135 customs-duty relief is to end by **October 2028** (announced 23 June 2026) (V).

## Ad-policy notes

- Ads follow the CAP Code (above) as well as the platform's rules; ASA rulings are public.
- Google Consent Mode applies to UK traffic (see `european-union.md`).
- BNPL in ads is a financial promotion (above).

## Page checklist

- [ ] Prices include VAT and every mandatory fee; delivery cost shown before checkout.
- [ ] "Was" prices genuine (CTSI guidance) and every deadline real.
- [ ] Returns page: 14-day cancellation, how to cancel (model form), who pays return
      postage, refund timing, the exceptions, and Consumer Rights Act remedies.
- [ ] Business name, geographic address, email and phone easy to find.
- [ ] Final button: "Order with obligation to pay", "Pay now" or equally clear.
- [ ] Cookie banner blocks ad pixels until consent; reject as easy as accept.
- [ ] Only real reviews; none hidden or bought.
- [ ] BNPL wording from the provider; subscriptions with clear terms and easy cancellation.
