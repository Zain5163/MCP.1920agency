## 1. Legality review of every request

Any request from a public authority (police, regulator, court, intelligence or
national-security body, in any country) for users' personal data is sent to the
owner. Nothing is disclosed until the owner has checked, in writing:

- who sent it, and that it really comes from that authority (contact them through
  an address found independently, not the one in the request);
- the law it relies on, and that the law applies to us and to the data asked for;
- that it is a binding legal order, not an informal request;
- whether a lawyer must review it (always, for national-security requests and for
  requests from outside Pakistan).

## 2. Challenging unlawful requests

If a request is unclear, too broad, not legally binding, or appears unlawful, we
ask the authority to clarify or narrow it, and we challenge it through the
available legal process before disclosing anything. Where the law allows, we tell
the affected user before disclosure so they can object.

## 3. Data minimisation

We disclose only the minimum data the order legally requires, for the named
users and dates only. Day to day we already keep little: post and ad content is
never sent to analytics; access tokens are stored encrypted (AES-256-GCM, key held
outside the database); call logs hold the tool name, outcome, timing and AI
client, never the content of calls (docs/architecture/2026-10-08-plans-usage-analytics-hosting.md,
"Privacy notes"). Data we do not hold cannot be disclosed.

## 4. Records

Every request is recorded in a private log, kept outside the code repository:
date received, authority, legal basis, data asked for, the review and its
reasoning, who decided, what (if anything) was disclosed and when, and any
challenge. Records are kept for at least 5 years.

## 5. Transparency

We do not answer Meta's question "Have you provided users' personal data to public
authorities in response to national security requests in the past 12 months?"
from memory: the record in section 4 is the source of the answer.
